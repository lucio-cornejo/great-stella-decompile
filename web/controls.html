New faces, fixed vertices

A faceting of a polyhedron uses its existing vertices but joins them into different face circuits. A pentagon becoming a pentagram is the planar version: the five points stay put while the boundary changes.

The construction used here

  1. Choose the base. Its canonical vertex set is the one the finished object must use.
  2. Find coplanar circuits. Maximal sets of three or more base vertices define candidate planes. Angular step orders generate ordinary polygons and single-component star polygons on those same points.
  3. Repeat by symmetry. The selected faceting group maps one seed circuit into a complete facet orbit. The polar dual's plane arrangement is shown alongside as the reciprocal faceting diagram.
  4. Validate the result. Every circuit must close and every undirected edge must normally meet exactly two faces. Open or multiply-shared edges are reported as non-tidy.

This is the geometric duality (stellation of P*)* = faceting of P. The arrangement code is reused, but the displayed vertices come from P, not from the stellation mesh. That distinction is what makes this a faceting port rather than a renamed stellation viewer.

Great Stella

The Great Stella shortcut uses the 20 vertices of the regular dodecahedron and replaces its twelve pentagons with twelve pentagram circuits. It is the great stellated dodecahedron shown in the brief. The base shortcut restores the twelve ordinary pentagons without moving a vertex.

Three linked views

3D faceting
The final abstract face cycles on the fixed base vertices. Edges are drawn from the cycles themselves, including pentagrams.
Faceting diagram
A representative plane of the reciprocal arrangement. Its regions explain the dual construction around one vertex orbit. This view is a read-only guide; selection happens in the orbit table or 3D view.
Construction table
Each numbered box is one symmetry orbit of explicit, coplanar base-vertex circuits. Its colour records how many faces it generates.

Controls

base
Use the original face orbit of the chosen solid.
next orbit / all / clear
Walk to the next circuit family, or select every/none of the directly enumerated facet orbits.
shift-click / ctrl-click
Add or remove one complete facet orbit. Plain clicks toggle it.
base symmetry / faceting symmetry
The first bounds the allowed subgroups and builds the reciprocal diagram; the second groups direct circuits into selectable orbits.
diagram depth
Limits reciprocal plane intersections in the read-only diagram. It does not change the base vertex set or direct circuit enumeration.

Validity and limits

The app keeps explicit face cycles and reports circuit and edge incidence checks. Self-intersecting star faces are intentional; repeated vertices within one circuit, open edges, and more than two faces on an abstract edge are not silently accepted as tidy.

Central reciprocal planes have points at infinity. When a source solid contains them the status line reports the dropped planes instead of presenting the depth-limited result as complete.

The plane-arrangement core derives from Vladimir Bulatov's Great Stella applet (1998–2001). This port adds the explicit polar-faceting model, circuit validation, and fixed-vertex presets.
