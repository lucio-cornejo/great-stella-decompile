Why the shapes coincide

The same-vertices / same-planes proof

01

Faceting fixes the icosahedron's vertices

Let φ=(1+√5)/2 and c=1/√(1+φ²). The app's unit-circumradius icosahedron is

VI = c · {(0, ±φ, ±1), (±φ, ±1, 0), (±1, 0, ±φ)}.

The direct engine copies this array by identity into every preview. It may replace the 20 triangular circuits, but it cannot add, move, merge, or delete a vertex.

02

Stellation fixes the dodecahedron's planes

The 12 outward unit normals of the dodecahedron's faces are exactly the 12 vectors in VI. Its normalized face planes are

ni · x = rD,   rD = √((5 + 2√5) / 15).

Stellation extends those planes indefinitely and chooses supported regions of the arrangement; the plane set does not change.

03

Orbit F2 satisfies both constraints at once

On each of 12 planes, the engine finds five points of VI. Connecting every second point makes a regular pentagram. The F2 support distance at the app's icosahedron scale is rF2=1/√5. Uniformly scaling the result by

λ = rD / rF2 = √((5 + 2√5) / 3) ≈ 1.7769014187

makes every F2 face plane coincide with a face plane of the original dodecahedron. So, up to the separate viewers' normalization, the F2 result uses the icosahedron's vertices and the extended dodecahedron's planes. That is the claimed shape equivalence.

A useful precision about duality

The icosahedron and dodecahedron are polar duals, which explains why dodecahedral face normals and icosahedral vertex directions are the same data. But the small stellated dodecahedron is not self-dual: its polar dual is the great dodecahedron. Calling the small stellated dodecahedron both a stellation and a faceting is a direct same-planes/same-vertices statement, not a claim that dualization leaves it unchanged.

How F2 is computed here

From 12 points to one complete pentagram orbit

  1. 1

    Normalize the fixed base

    Read U27, the regular icosahedron: 12 vertices and 20 triangular faces. The faces seed the “base” preset, but only the vertex table is immutable during faceting.

  2. 2

    Enumerate coplanar vertex sets

    Test the C(12,3)=220 vertex triples. Canonicalize each nondegenerate plane and merge numerically equal planes. The icosahedral table yields 67 distinct planes. All base vertices lying within the plane tolerance are collected.

  3. 3

    Generate closed angular-step circuits

    Project each coplanar set into a local 2D basis, sort around its centroid, and walk by constant step k. A step is a single circuit when gcd(n,k)=1. Ordinary and star traversals together produce 79 explicit circuits.

  4. 4

    Quotient by icosahedral symmetry

    Map the circuits through the 60 proper rotations of group I and canonicalize their vertex cycles. This partitions the 79 candidates into the five selectable orbits F0–F4 shown below.

  5. 5

    Select F2 and validate globally

    Seed circuit [1,8,0,6,3] is a five-point, step-2 star. Its orbit has 12 faces. Build all 12 at once, deduplicate the undirected circuit edges, and verify that every edge has two incident faces and every fixed vertex participates.

Why V − E + F = −6 is not an error

For F2 the raw incidence count is 12 − 30 + 12 = −6. Euler's familiar value 2 assumes the boundary of a convex ball (or, more generally, a sphere). Here each face is a self-intersecting pentagram and faces pass through one another. The engine's “tidy” check asks the relevant circuit question—does every abstract edge belong to exactly two faces?—and that check passes. Apparent line crossings inside a pentagram are not extra vertices or edges.

How the stellation side is computed

Face planes divide space into supported cells

supported boundarycore + first shell exposes pentagrams

Bulatov defines a cell's layer number as the number of stellation planes crossed by a ray from the core to an interior point of that cell. Layer 0 is the core. A cell is supported when every lower facet has an adjacent selected cell. The “+ layer” operation therefore adds a complete shell, not merely a set of disconnected spikes. Reading the boundary of the core-plus-first-shell selection gives the same 12 pentagram supporting planes certified above.

This is why the two algorithms look so different. Stellation computes bounded volumes in a plane arrangement and then extracts their boundary. Direct faceting computes coplanar circuits on a fixed point set. They converge here because dodecahedral planes and icosahedral vertex directions are reciprocal.
